SARVARC Forms: The Form Builder Where Your Data Never Dead-Ends

Now with a Gallery that sends your images onto the form, so the person filling it in sees your work first.

By the SARVARC team · Published September 15, 2026 · Updated November 5, 2026

Many form builders do one job: collect responses and dump them into a spreadsheet you then have to open somewhere else. That works fine until you need those responses in a report, or matched back against a customer list you already had. That's when you start exporting CSVs, re-importing them into another tool, and manually fixing headers that don't quite match.

SARVARC Forms was built to remove that gap, and it now goes one step further: the form itself makes a first impression, because your own images can be sent onto it. Here is what that means in practice, and why it matters if you run a business.

Key takeaways
  • Gallery: your own images, sent onto the form for the receiver to see.
  • First impressions: proof of your work comes before the first question.
  • Flow Forms: one question at a time, built for surveys.
  • Closed loop: responses, tables, and pages move in and out of the workspace.

SARVARC Forms, live inside the Workspace editor

What Is SARVARC Forms?

SARVARC Forms is an online form builder inside SARVARC Workspace, where you build, brand, and publish forms and keep working with the responses without leaving the workspace.

How Does SARVARC Forms Publish a Branded Public Form?

SARVARC Forms publishes every form as a public, shareable URL that carries your actual branding, not just a plain survey link: your logo with an adjustable watermark, a festive or campaign banner image, your accent color, your social links, and attachment folders such as brochures, price lists, or terms and conditions that a respondent can open right from the live form.

This means the form you send to a client or post for customers looks like your business, not a generic survey. You do not need a separate landing-page tool just to make a form collection point look presentable.

What Is the Gallery in SARVARC Forms?New

The Gallery is a built-in way to put your own images in front of the person filling in your form. You upload up to 8 photos of past projects, products, places, or people in the editor, and when you publish, SARVARC Forms sends them onto the live form, where the receiver sees them as a 3D gallery. Images can be any size or shape, and each one keeps its own proportions, so a tall poster and a wide landscape photo both look right.

In a Flow Form, the gallery opens right after the welcome screen. Once the person starts answering, it eases back behind the question: on wide screens it drifts to the right of the question, and on phones it sits softly dimmed behind it. A small "View our work" button brings the full gallery back at any time, and the slideshow pauses while someone is typing so it never competes with their answer.

How Does a Form Gallery Help You Win Clients?

A gallery helps you win clients because it puts proof of your work in front of a prospect at the exact moment they are deciding whether to trust you. A plain enquiry form asks for their time and their details with nothing in return. A form that opens with your best projects gives them a reason to continue.

Your form is often the first thing a prospect experiences. It should look like your best work, not like a questionnaire.

The B2B value: a professional first impression

For agencies, contractors, consultants, manufacturers, and any business that sells to other businesses, the first interaction usually decides whether you are taken seriously. The Gallery works as a portfolio and a lead form in one step:

  • Proof before the first question. A prospect sees completed projects, case-study visuals, or product lines while they are still deciding whether to reach out.
  • Trust and credibility. A branded form with real work behind it signals an established business, which matters when the buyer is comparing several suppliers.
  • Better-qualified enquiries. When people see the kind of work you do, the ones who continue may be a better fit, and your qualifying questions land on interested prospects.
  • One link does two jobs. You can send the same URL in a proposal, an email, or a chat message and it works as both your portfolio and your intake.

The B2C value: customers see what they are getting

For salons, bakeries, photographers, studios, tutors, gyms, and home-service providers, people want to see the result before they book. The gallery lets them do that inside the booking or enquiry itself:

  • Less hesitation. A customer who has just seen your real work is more comfortable handing over their name and number.
  • An engaging experience. Moving, tactile images make a form feel like part of your brand, not a chore, so people are more willing to finish it.
  • Seasonal and campaign ready. Swap in new images for a festival offer, a new collection, or a launch, and the same form instantly feels current.

Can a Gallery Increase Form Conversion Rates?

It can help, but SARVARC does not promise a fixed percentage lift. The effect depends on your offer, your audience, and the quality of your images. The gallery changes the one thing it controls: what a visitor sees before the first question. If you want to know what it does for you, publish it on one form and compare how many people finish it before and after.

Why Are Most Online Forms So Generic?

Many online forms look the same because many form builders optimise for speed of creation, not for how the form feels. A white page, a stack of boxes, and a submit button is the default everywhere, so your form looks identical to every competitor's and feels like paperwork to the person filling it in.

If you really need a survey, use Flow Forms. A long, single-page survey is exactly the kind of form regular people abandon, while one question at a time reads like a short conversation instead of a test.

What makes Flow Forms different?

  • One question at a time with a progress bar, so people always know how far along they are.
  • Lettered answer options you can pick by pressing A, B, or C, and Enter to continue, which makes answering fast on a laptop and easy on a phone.
  • 20 ready-made templates with animated backgrounds, such as Aurora, Midnight, Ocean, and Cosmos, each with its own way of bringing in the next question.
  • Logo-matched colors. Templates can pick up your logo's colors, so the form feels like your brand and not a stock theme.

How Do I Add a Gallery to My SARVARC Form?

Adding a gallery takes five steps in Make Forms:

  1. Open Make Forms in SARVARC Workspace and build or open your form.
  2. Choose Flow under Form look in the Design tab, and pick a template if you like.
  3. Open the Gallery section and upload your images. You can give the gallery an optional heading, such as "Our recent work", and add a short caption to each image.
  4. Choose the glow: Off, Subtle, or Full, depending on how much of your template's colors you want shining through the images.
  5. Publish and share the link. Check the Live Preview first.

How Do Form Responses Become Usable Data?

SARVARC Forms turns responses into a structured table in one click. Responses from your published form come back into the Fill & Responses tab, and Export to Data Arrangement converts every saved response: field labels become column headers, checkbox answers convert to Yes or No, and rows run from oldest to newest. From there you are one more click from a chart, a diagram, or a section in a branded report.

The response data was never really trapped inside the form to begin with.

Can I Import Existing Spreadsheet Data Into a Form?

Yes. Generate from Table works like a mail merge: it takes an existing Data Arrangement table and saves one response per row into a form. If you already have a customer list, an inventory sheet, or vendor data sitting in a spreadsheet, you do not have to re-enter it as individual submissions.

The mapping step handles the tedious part automatically. It matches your form's field labels against your spreadsheet's column headers by name, ignoring capital letters, spaces, and symbols, so a field called "Full Name" lines up with a column called "Name" or "full_name" without any manual selection. Fields it cannot match are left blank, and you can change any match before importing. This is useful for pre-populating a form with known customer data, generating personalized document batches, or migrating existing records into a form-based workflow.

What If My Form Needs to Match an Existing Paper Layout?

Use a canvas form: free-placement fillable text boxes on a blank A4 page, instead of a question-by-question list. SARVARC Forms keeps its field-based builder for that list, so you pick the style per form.

This matters when you are digitizing something with a fixed layout, such as an official application or an existing paper form your business has used for years. Instead of reshaping your process to fit a generic question list, you place fillable fields exactly where they belong on the page you already know.

Does the Form Output Stay Locked Inside the Forms Module?

No. You can send a form into the PDF Editor as A4 pages with an editable text box placed on every field, and the workspace remembers those pages came from a form. When you later run Refine Report on a document, it leaves those form pages exactly as you pushed them and only restyles pages you added afterward, so a layout you built on purpose is not overwritten. Your form is not a dead end that only produces a response table. Its pages can become part of a finished document too.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I add a gallery to a standard form as well?

Yes. On a standard single-page form your images appear as a strip above the questions, instead of the full 3D gallery that Flow Forms show.

What images can I use in the Gallery?

JPG, PNG, WebP, or GIF. Each image is resized to 1280 pixels on its longest side and saved as WebP, so the gallery stays light to load. Most browsers cannot read HEIC photos, so export those as JPG or PNG first.

How do I change the gallery on a form that is already live?

Edit the images in the Gallery section, then click Update Link on the form. The live form keeps showing the old images until you do.

Can a form close by itself?

Yes. Set Form Expiration to stop accepting responses automatically after a time you choose, or leave it on Never expires. If it has expired, Renew now reopens it.

Can people sign a form online?

Yes. A signature field lets people sign right on the published form by drawing, typing, or uploading an image of their signature.

The Pattern Underneath All of This

None of these features are flashy on their own. What makes them add up is that none of them require leaving SARVARC or touching a CSV: your form, your images, your responses, your existing data, and your documents all stay in one workspace. It is a closed loop, not a funnel that ends the moment someone hits submit, and closing that gap is why SARVARC Forms exists.
